Question

Three single-agent (SA) policies exist: tok3, the big token model, and the model trained with constraints as terminations (CaT). What does each policy see, what action does it give, which reward trains it, and how does it perform in test? How does the CaT policy go to the multi-agent (MA) problem?

A clean flight reaches its goal with 0 intrusions, 0 restricted-area entries and 0 sector exits. An intrusion is another aircraft within 5 NM.

2 · Inputs

The policy sees only its own aircraft. It has two input blocks, each with the last 4 decision steps.

  • Lidar. 120 rays around the aircraft (360°), range 50 km. A ray gives the distance to its nearest hit: 1 − distance / 50 km. There are 4 channels: restricted area, sector boundary, other aircraft (5 NM disc), and the closing speed of the aircraft on that ray. A ray that hits the own goal gives 0.
  • Own state. 8 values per step: airspeed, inside-sector flag, cos and sin of the angle to the goal, distance to the goal, last turn action, last speed action, inside-area flag.

The encoder changes these inputs into tokens. The lidar tokens see near objects. The other tokens give exact values that the rays do not give.

3 · Action

  • Output. 2 numbers in [−1, 1]: turn and speed. One decision each 10 s. The actor is a small MLP (64, 64) on the encoder output. Training samples from a Gaussian around the mean; the test uses the mean.
  • Speed. Speed change = action × 6.67 m/s per decision (environment value d_speed = 20/3). Same in all three models.
  • Turn, tok3 and big token. Heading change = action × 45° per decision (d_heading). The policy must find the goal direction itself.
  • Turn, CaT model (goal map). Target heading = bearing to the goal + action × 90°. A wrapper sends the heading change to that target, limited to ±45° per decision. Action 0 flies to the goal. The policy cannot command a heading away from the goal by more than 90°.

5 · Training with CaT

  • Rule. An avoidable violation is a new intrusion, a restricted-area entry or a sector exit. The first one ends the training flight and adds −100. An intrusion that exists at the start of the flight does not count, because the policy cannot avoid it.
  • Probability. The code can end a flight with a probability below 1 (random CaT). These runs use probability 1: each violation ends the flight.
  • Effect on the data. A flight after a violation gives no more steps. The policy thus trains only on states with no violation before them.
  • Failure replay. The scenario seed of a failed flight goes into a pool (256 seeds at most per worker). 30 % of the resets fly a pool seed again. A seed leaves the pool after a clean flight or after 3 replays.
  • Test. CaT is off in test. A test flight continues after a violation, and the official metrics count all of it.
  • PPO. Same as before: lr 5e-4, γ 0.99, λ 0.95, clip 0.2, rollout 512 steps, 2 epochs, batch 128, no entropy term. Actor and critic share the encoder.
  • Source of CaT. Chane-Sane et al. 2024, arXiv:2403.18765, as cited in rl_tf/envs.py. DOI not verified.

Setup

  • SA environment. Competition SA env: 1 controlled aircraft, 10 scripted intruders, 5 restricted areas, 3000 s limit, 10 s per decision.
  • MA environment. Competition MA env: 10 controlled aircraft, 5 restricted areas, 3000 s limit.
  • Test protocol. scripts.competition_eval: 1000 episodes of one scenario seed, deterministic policy. SA: 1000 flights. MA: 10,000 aircraft flights.
  • Seeds. Seed 42 is the leaderboard test seed. Seed 7 is the validation seed. tok3 has a seed-42 result only (its model file is not on this machine). The CaT models have no SA result on seed 42, because seed 42 is kept for the test.
  • Model seeds. One training seed (0) per model.

What the data supports

  • tok3 → big token (seed 42). Clean flights go from 57.8 % to 61.2 %. Intrusion events go down (0.441 → 0.293). Restricted-area events and time go up (0.134 → 0.181; 7.7 s → 19.4 s).
  • CaT at 4 M steps (seed 7). Clean flights are 68.2 % against 57.7 % for the big token model. This is 10.5 points, about 4.9 standard errors (unpaired binomial, 1000 flights each). Sector exits go from 0.060 to 0.017.
  • MA. The CaT model has 0.02 intrusion events per flight in MA against 0.30 in SA. Restricted-area events are at the same level in both (0.13 to 0.20).

What the data does not support

  • CaT at equal steps is not better. At 2 M steps the CaT model has 58.6 % against 57.7 % (0.9 points, less than one standard error). Its goal share is lower (96.8 % against 99.6 %), its flights are 212 s longer, and its area time is 3.6 times higher.
  • The gain at 4 M steps is not proven to come from CaT. The CaT model at 4 M has twice the training steps of the big token model. No big token model with 4 M steps exists. The CaT model also changes four things at one time: action map, margin costs, CaT end and failure replay.
  • A plain fine-tune is as good. On the first 200 flights of seed 7, the fine-tune sa_tf_big_ftpen10_s0 (big token model + 1 M steps, lr 1e-4, −10 per event, no CaT) has 148 clean flights (74.0 %). The CaT model at 4 M has 143 (71.5 %). The big token model has 112 (56.0 %).
  • Cause of the low MA intrusion count. The data does not separate the two differences between SA and MA: the other aircraft are agents and not scripted intruders, and the start conditions differ.

Limits

  • One training seed per model. The spread over training seeds is not known.
  • The tables give means over flights with no standard deviation. The standard deviations are in the result files.
  • tok3 against the CaT model: not matched. They have no result on the same seed.
  • The fine-tune comparison uses 200 flights only, and its model file is not on this machine.
  • The SA and MA values come from different environments. A direct comparison of the clean share (68.2 % against 90.35 %) is not matched.
  • Status EXPLORATORY: these results come from evaluations, not from a planned experiment.

Next step

Fine-tune sa_v2m_s0_c2 in the MA environment and measure it on the same seed-7 episodes. Before the run, choose the safety signal for MA: event penalties, or CaT per aircraft with a code change. For a clean test of CaT in SA, train the big token model to 4 M steps with no CaT and compare it with the CaT model on seed 7.
